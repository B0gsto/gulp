# Avatar-free Roblox games endure, seldom break out

Building a Roblox game where the player is a creature, ball or vehicle instead of a Roblox avatar is a workable idea in October 2026, but the format itself gives you no edge, so treat it as a product choice and keep the first build small. Four animal and creature games each hold roughly **9,000 to 38,000 concurrent players and 0.6 to 2.2 billion lifetime visits**, which proves players accept losing their avatar. All of them predate 2025, and the research found **no non-avatar game launched in 2025 or 2026 that passed 10,000 concurrent players**; the new entrants it could confirm peaked at about 2,000 or fewer. Roblox's discovery system rewards retention and says nothing about art style or avatar use, so a different look is neither penalised nor rewarded. The engine supports a character with no Humanoid, and the official Studio MCP server lets Claude Code drive Studio on a Mac, but you must rebuild camera, input, health, respawn and animation yourself. The money is thin for almost everyone: the median creator enrolled in Developer Exchange reportedly earned about **$1,500 in the year to June 2026**. The recommended first game is a food-chain arena (eat, grow, bank, unlock new species), built to a narrow MVP and judged on day-1 retention and session time before any further investment. Much of the market data below comes from third-party trackers and search snippets, and the report marks each inference as its own.

Two results in this table are better sourced than the rest. Coolabi, the rights holder for Warrior Cats, stated in May 2023 that the game had **300 million visits, more than 2 million unique players a month, and a top-100 rank for playtime and retention** ([Coolabi](https://coolabi.com/warrior-cats-ultimate-edition-roblox-game-hits-300-million-game-visits-on-its-second-anniversary/)). The studio behind Creatures of Sonaria merged into Twin Atlas and signed a TV adaptation deal, which shows an original Roblox creature world can become licensable IP ([Pryor Cashman](https://www.pryorcashman.com/film-tv-podcast/news/pryor-cashman-client-twin-atlas-signs-roblox-tv-series-deal)). Neither is a solo-developer story: Sonar Studios was a multi-title group, and Warrior Cats rides a book licence. The research could not confirm team size, revenue or monetisation mix for any title in the table.

The table also shows a split by character type. Creature games with a persistent world sit in the thousands to tens of thousands of concurrent players. Pure ball and marble games reached tens of millions of lifetime visits over many years but hold almost no one today. Driving games are large (Driving Empire shows about 23,800 concurrent players and 3.4 billion visits on a fetched tracker page) but are 2019-era incumbents, and the research did not confirm that any of them hides the avatar ([Rotrends](https://rotrends.com/game/1202096104/Driving-Empire-NEW-LIMITED)).

The weakest part of the picture is recent launches. The 2025 megahits were avatar-based and looked like Roblox: Grow a Garden peaked near 22.3 million concurrent players in August 2025 and left the top 10 by December ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)), and Steal a Brainrot passed 25 million in October 2025 before falling to about 215,000 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot), [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/is-the-brainrot-trend-dying-what-ccu-data-from-three-top-games-reveals)). Among non-avatar launches, the largest figures found were small: Trevor Henderson Creatures (May 2025) peaked at 393 ([Rotrends](https://rotrends.com/game/6240778564)), Snake Game (November 2025) at 673 ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/9260916865/snake-game)), and the older Snake Simulator at about 2,000 ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/6170013953/snake-simulator)). These come from search snippets, and the researchers did not pull Roblox's own charts, so a recent non-avatar breakout could exist and have been missed. Launch dates for Dragon Adventures, Feather Family and Wild Horse Islands were also not found, though the trackers show all the incumbents predate 2025. On the evidence in hand, the format's successes are older games that built their audience before the current market.

## Dropping the avatar costs identity and item sales, not discovery

### Discovery rewards retention and ignores how the game looks

Roblox's Creator Hub names the signals that drive recommendations: play-through rate from impressions first, then play days per user, playtime per user (capped at 60 minutes per user per game per day), and first-play bounce rate measured under 60 seconds and at 61-180 seconds ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/discovery)). The documentation does not mention art style, visual fidelity or avatar use. The Q2 2026 shareholder letter adds that the system "intentionally provides more impressions for highly retentive games at the expense of near term monetization" ([Roblox Q2 2026 letter, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001315098/000162828026051059/ex991-robloxq22026earnin.htm)). Press coverage of the September 2026 developer conference reports that rankings will use data beyond the first 28 days ([Invenglobal](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/25918/roblox-unveils-expansions-to-play-creation-and-monetization-at-rdc)).

This report's inference is that the non-avatar choice is neutral for discovery. A game that holds players for long sessions over many days gets impressions whatever it looks like, and the creature sims' profile (long-lived, high playtime) suits the current weighting. The other half of that inference matters just as much: the research found **no evidence that a non-Roblox look improves click-through or retention**. Nobody has published a comparison of stylised and Roblox-looking games.

### Monetisation loses avatar items and keeps everything else

Without a visible avatar you cannot sell avatar items in the game, and the research found no figure for how much revenue that line is worth to a typical game. What remains is game passes, developer products, private servers, subscriptions, rewarded video ads and Roblox's Creator Rewards. The official exchange rate is **$0.0038 per Earned Robux**, with a 30,000 Robux minimum (about $114) and one cash-out a month; a higher $0.0054 rate applies only to certain purchases by US players verified as 18 or older ([Roblox DevEx](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/monetization/developer-exchange.md)). At the standard rate, $1,000 a month takes about 263,000 Earned Robux a month (our arithmetic).

Creator Rewards pays 5 Robux, about $0.02, for each "Active Spender" who plays your game for 10 minutes or more in a day, provided it is one of the first three games they launch that day. A second programme pays 35% of a new or returning user's first $100 of purchases, but only for games averaging 100 or more daily users ([Roblox Creator Rewards](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/creator-rewards)). Rewarded video is available, but the only earnings figures are third-party: $6-12 per 1,000 views with a reported 30% creator share, which would mean roughly $2-4 per 1,000 views to you ([ROLearn](https://rolearn.dev/trend-reports/roblox-monetization-trends-devex-creator-rewards)). Treat that as a number to test.

Loot boxes carry real compliance work. Roblox requires every outcome and its odds shown as percentages that total exactly 100%, including when the player spends in-game currency, and requires a per-player check of `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted` with an alternative path where they are restricted ([Roblox paid random items](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items)).

The income distribution is the sobering fact. Press coverage of Roblox's 2026 figures reports about $1.7 billion paid through Developer Exchange in the year to June 2026, with the top 1,000 creators averaging $1.4 million and the **median of more than 42,000 enrolled creators earning about $1,500** ([Says](https://says.com/my/tech/roblox-creators-earn-1-7-billion-developer-payouts)). That page was read as a search snippet only. No official revenue-per-visit, revenue-per-daily-user or payer-conversion benchmark was found for any genre.

### The audience is older than its reputation and growing outside North America

Roblox reported 123 million daily users in Q2 2026, up 10% year on year; secondary coverage puts the peak at 152 million in Q3 2025 ([Roblox Q2 2026 letter, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001315098/000162828026051059/ex991-robloxq22026earnin.htm), [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/150205/roblox-rblx-q2-2026-earnings-results)). US and Canada daily users grew 6% and hours 1%, while Japan grew 67% and India 64%. A secondary summary of the Q4 2025 letter says that of age-checked users, 35% are under 13, 38% are 13-17 and 27% are 18 or older; the letter itself was not opened ([Roblox Q4 2025 letter](https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Q4-2025-Shareholder-Letter.pdf)). The Q1 2026 letter, seen through a search summary, says adult users spend over 50% more than under-18s ([Roblox Q1 2026 letter](https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_financials/2026/q1/Q1-2026-Earnings-Shareholder-Letter.pdf)). No device split was found.

Since January 2026 every user must pass an age check to chat, and chat is limited to adjacent age groups ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/07/roblox-now-requires-all-users-globally-to-complete-age-checks-to-access-chat)). The Q1 2026 letter, via the same search summary, says this reduced communication, sign-ups and daily users. This report's inference: roleplay-heavy creature sims, which lean on text chat, are more exposed to this change than games where players interact through mechanics, and a game with little text also travels better to the fast-growing Japanese and Indian audiences.

### What players lose is unmeasured

The research found no data on whether players dislike games that replace their avatar. The nearest evidence is the backlash when Roblox itself retired classic avatar faces, which shows players care about avatar identity ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/roblox/roblox-under-fire-for-removing-classic-avatars-2226960/)). That is adjacent evidence, and the long life of the creature sims points the other way. The practical reading is that a non-avatar game must give players another form of identity to own, such as species, skins and patterns, which is also where its revenue comes from.

## The engine allows it, and you own everything the Humanoid did

### A character without a Humanoid works, with a rebuild list

A player's character can be any Model. The clearest statement comes from a 2023 DevForum reply, not official documentation: "Player.Character has to be a Model... but it does not need to contain a Humanoid" ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/does-a-player-have-to-be-a-humanoid/2572541)). The official page on custom characters returned a 404 during research. The same thread lists what you take on: set `Players.CharacterAutoLoads` to false, assign the model in script, drive the camera yourself, code all input including touch, write your own health, and play animations through an AnimationController. Other DevForum threads report specific traps with this setup: spawns landing at the world origin, interface elements missing until the first character load, and a custom ball character that would not respawn until the developer wired it up by hand ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/custom-character-wont-respawn/2807595)).

Roblox's two newer systems look helpful but should not be load-bearing yet. The Character Controller Library is labelled beta on one official page and is documented around Humanoid properties, so its behaviour on a non-Humanoid character is unknown ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/characters/character-controller-library/controllers)). Server Authority, Roblox's server-validated movement system, has a DevForum post titled "[Full Release]" while a third-party article calls it a client beta, and nobody confirmed whether it covers fully custom physics ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/full-release-ship-fair-and-competitive-games-with-server-authority/4727993)). Roblox's security guidance is unambiguous that client-side checks can be bypassed and validation belongs on the server ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/scripting/security/security-tactics)). This report's recommendation is to write a simple custom controller with server-side sanity checks on speed and position, and to test both newer systems in a one-week spike before relying on either.

Input and interface are on firmer ground. The Input Action System gives one layer for keyboard, gamepad and touch, switched on with a Workspace property ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/input/input-action-system.md)). `StarterGui:SetCoreGuiEnabled` covers the player list, health bar, backpack, chat and emotes menu ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/CoreGuiType)). The Esc menu and F9 console are reserved by the engine, so a Roblox game cannot be made to hide Roblox entirely. Whether chat bubbles, voice chat, proximity prompts, tools and seats work without a Humanoid was not verified and needs testing in Studio.

### Blender art fits within clear limits

Roblox's general mesh specification, fetched directly, sets **20,000 triangles per mesh, at most 4 bone influences per vertex, and one animation track per exported file** ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/specifications)). Forum posts quote 21,000 triangles; use the official figure. A third-party guide recommends staying under 10,000 per mesh for mobile ([Nilo](https://nilo.io/articles/vibe-code-roblox-triangle-limit)). Texture size limits, PBR map support, upload fees, moderation delays and the Blender-to-Studio import workflow were not researched. Custom shaders were not confirmed on any official page; assume they are unavailable and get the non-Roblox look from meshes, textures, lighting, post-processing and a fully custom interface. Advice that the highest-quality lighting mode is too heavy for phones comes from a single secondary snippet.

The cost difference between character types is this report's inference, and it shapes the recommendation. A ball or simple vehicle is a rigid body with no rig and no animation. A fish or blob needs one or two simple animations. A four-legged creature needs a rig, a walk cycle, turning, idle and attack animations per species, which is the most expensive art a solo developer can sign up for.

### The Mac toolchain is the best-verified part of the plan

Roblox's official Studio MCP server runs on macOS and lists **Claude Code as a quick-connect client**. Through it an agent can read and edit scripts, search the data model, run Luau in edit, client or server contexts, start and stop play mode, read console output, take screenshots and simulate input ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/studio/mcp)). Rojo, the tool that syncs files on disk into Studio, is at v7.7.1, and v7.7.0 fixed a macOS path bug ([Rojo releases](https://github.com/rojo-rbx/rojo/releases)). Open Cloud Luau Execution runs scripts headlessly against a published place, for up to 5 minutes per task and 10 tasks at once ([Roblox Open Cloud](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/cloud/reference/features/luau-execution.md)). Publishing a place from the command line was not verified, and neither were the linters, type checker and test frameworks. Studio must stay open for live sync, so on a 16 GB Mac it competes with Blender for memory.

## Underserved-genre claims rest on dated or unverified data

No 2026 genre report from Roblox was found. The most recent official data is from July 2024, when Roblox published first-quarter 2024 search volumes: about 14 million for open-world action, 13.5 million for sports, 12.5 million for driving and racing, and 7.5 million for social co-opetition, with Creatures of Sonaria named as a success ([Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2024/07/roblox-genre-insights-what-will-you-create-next)). At GDC 2026 Roblox reportedly called RPG, strategy and shooter games "heavily underrepresented" relative to demand from older players ([tbreak](https://tbreak.com/roblox-incubator-jumpstart-creator-programs/)). A third-party report claims music, rhythm and educational games are under-supplied but names no games and gives no method ([ROLearn](https://rolearn.dev/trend-reports/emerging-roblox-game-genres-q2-2026)).

The research notes disagree on the one genre that matters most here, the eat-and-grow loop of Hungry Shark and Slither.io. One researcher called it a gap, because no high-quality Roblox version was found and Hungry Shark passed a billion mobile downloads ([GamesPress](https://gamespress.com/HUNGRY-SHARK-FRANCHISE-HITS-ONE-BILLION-DOWNLOADS-ON-MOBILE-PLATFORMS)). Another found that Roblox versions do exist and stay small: Eat Blobs to Grow MASSIVE peaked at 204 players, Be a Shark at 703, Be a Snake at about 1,000 ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/6515096150/eat-blobs-to-grow-massive), [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/5289155961/be-a-shark)). Both readings fit the data. Either the loop does not hold Roblox players, or the existing versions are thin and lack long-term progression. Nobody inspected those games' quality, so the "gap" is a hypothesis you would be testing with your own launch.

For a solo developer the field narrows on cost. Creature survival has proven demand but incumbents with years of species content. Brainrot-style collection games spike and collapse within weeks, and clones peaked around 3,000 players ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/9590247304/stealing-from-brainrots)). Driving has large incumbents and needs big vehicle catalogues. Marble racing is cheap and has the lowest evidenced ceiling. No postmortem from a solo developer of a 2025-2026 hit was found, so the scope estimates below are this report's judgment.

## Three concepts, and why the food-chain arena goes first

The three concepts below are original designs by this report, built on genre mechanics. Their loops, feature lists and monetisation plans are proposals; only the platform rules they lean on are verified. The research found no price benchmarks for species or skins, so prices should come from live testing.

| | A. Food-chain arena | B. Small-world creature survival | C. Marble racing party game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Character | Aquatic creature that grows | Small animal with a life cycle | Rolling ball |
| Demand evidence | Unproven on Roblox; strong on mobile | Strongest: incumbents at 3K-38K | Weakest: old marble games near zero |
| Art cost | Low to medium | High (rigs and animation sets) | Lowest (no rigs) |
| Dependence on chat | Low | High | Low |
| Main risk | The loop fails to retain | Content race against incumbents | Low ceiling |

### Concept A (top pick): a food-chain arena where you eat, grow and bank

The player is a small aquatic creature in a shared pond or reef with 20 to 30 others. You eat things smaller than you, grow through visible size tiers, and avoid anything larger. Growth is lost on death unless you reach a safe zone and bank it as permanent currency, which unlocks new species with different movement abilities. The core loop runs spawn, eat, grow, choose between banking and pushing on, die or cash out, unlock, respawn. A run lasts 5 to 15 minutes, which clears the 10-minute Creator Rewards threshold and stays under the 60-minute daily playtime cap in the discovery signals.

The MVP is one map with three depth zones; five species, each a single mesh that scales with size plus one mid-run form change; computer-controlled prey and one predator type so the world works on a half-empty server; a custom swim controller with server-side speed and eat validation; a custom camera; touch, keyboard and gamepad input; a custom interface with the default Roblox elements disabled; saved progress; a server leaderboard; three daily goals; and a first-minute tutorial that gets the player eating within seconds, because bounce is measured in the first 60. Swimming removes ground contact, climbing and jumping from the controller, and fish need only a tail animation, which keeps both code and art small.

Monetisation uses direct purchases only at launch: premium species and colour patterns sold individually, a game pass for a permanent bonus to banked currency, a developer product that revives you once per run at your current size, private servers, and a rewarded video as the free alternative to the paid revive (rewarded video is built for extra lives and currency per press coverage of the format, [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/roblox-expands-rewarded-video-tools-creators-advertisers)). Skip loot-box eggs until the game has an audience, which avoids the odds-disclosure and regional-restriction work.

This concept goes first because it has the cheapest non-trivial character, the shortest path to a testable loop, no reliance on chat, and little text to localise. Its weakness is stated above: existing Roblox games with this loop are small, and the bet is that persistent species progression and better execution change that.

### Concept B: a small-world creature survival sim

The player is a small animal in one dense habitat, such as a hedgerow or tide pool, growing from juvenile to adult over real time while managing food, water and predators, nesting and forming packs with other players. This is the proven model of Creatures of Sonaria and Wild Horse Islands at a deliberately small scale. The loop is survive, grow, raise or join a pack, unlock the next species. The MVP is one map, four species with full animation sets, hunger and thirst, a growth timer, nests, a party system and an emote wheel so players can communicate without text chat. Monetisation is direct sale of species and skins, extra save slots, and private servers for roleplay groups.

Demand evidence is strongest here and so is the cost. Every species needs a rig and several animations, the incumbents have years of content, and the 2025 creature-survival launch the research found peaked at 393 players. Build this second, reusing the controller, camera and interface from Concept A, if A shows that players stay for the creatures.

### Concept C: a marble racing party game

Eight to sixteen players roll balls through short obstacle courses in elimination rounds. The loop is race, place, earn, customise, queue again. The MVP is six tracks, a round and matchmaking system, a ball physics controller, time-trial leaderboards and cosmetic skins and trails. Monetisation is cosmetics sold directly, private servers, and rewarded video for cosmetic currency. This is the cheapest to build and the safest technically, and the evidence for its ceiling is the weakest: the long-running marble games hold almost no players. One recent signal, a November 2025 game called Build a Marble Slide! with a reported 1.5 million favourites, was seen only in a search snippet with no player count ([Rotrends](https://rotrends.com/game/9150975254)). Treat C as the fallback if the creature controller proves harder than expected.

### How to judge the first build

Roblox's analytics show how your retention and session time compare with similar games as percentiles, but only once the game has 100 or more daily users ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/analytics)). Its guidance, seen in a search snippet of the same page, is to get day-1 retention and session time right before paying for traffic. Ads Manager has no stated minimum budget on the fetched page beyond a $5 first card charge, and the audiences that target recent or lapsed players need 10,000 or more of them, so a new game buys broad traffic ([Roblox Ads Manager](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/ads-manager)). This report's proposed gate: buy just enough traffic to cross 100 daily users, read the percentiles, and continue only if day-1 retention and session time sit at or above the median for similar games. No cost-per-play figure was found, so the price of that test is unknown until you run it.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Whether Roblox players will accept a game without their avatar is settled: they have, for years, in the billions of visits. What nobody has shown is that a new non-avatar game can still find an audience in the 2025-2026 market, where the breakouts were avatar-based social games and the recommendation system now pays for retention over spectacle. That makes the first build an experiment with a clear readout, and the right design is the one that reaches that readout for the least art and code. The food-chain arena does that, and its controller, camera and interface carry over to the creature sim if the numbers justify going further.

One lead deserves a follow-up before you commit. Coverage of the September 2026 developer conference reports that Roblox plans standalone game apps and Chrome-based play ([91mobiles](https://www.91mobiles.com/gaming/roblox-announces-standalone-game-apps-at-rdc-2026/)). If a Roblox game can ship under its own icon, a game that looks nothing like Roblox gains a reason to exist that today's evidence cannot measure. The terms, eligibility and timing were not researched. Three checks would firm up the rest: pull Roblox's own charts for recent non-avatar launches, play the existing eat-and-grow games to see why they stay small, and run the one-week Studio spike on a non-Humanoid swimming character with the MCP toolchain.
